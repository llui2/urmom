\title{Adaptive response repertoires}
\maketitle

\section{Results}

The classical law of requisite variety relates successful regulation to the range of responses available to a regulator \cite{ashby1956}. A related idea appears in ecological response diversity, where components contributing to the same present function can differ in how they respond to environmental change \cite{elmqvist2003}. We start from the minimal continuous setting in which these ideas can be made geometric and dynamical.

\subsection{Response repertoire}

Consider a finite repertoire of $n$ response vectors
\begin{equation}
\mathbf a_i\in\mathbb R^d,
\qquad i=1,\ldots,n,
\end{equation}
with nonnegative weights $p_i$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
p_i\geq0,
\qquad
\sum_i p_i=1.
\label{eq:simplex}
\end{equation}
The realized response is the weighted mean
\begin{equation}
\mathbf r
=
\sum_i p_i\mathbf a_i.
\label{eq:response}
\end{equation}
The vectors $\mathbf a_i$ are fixed in the present model; adaptation only redistributes the available weight among them.

Let $\mathbf y(t)\in\mathbb R^d$ denote the response required by the current environment and define the quadratic mismatch
\begin{equation}
E
=
\frac12\|\mathbf r-\mathbf y\|^2.
\label{eq:error}
\end{equation}
We let the weights descend this mismatch while remaining on the simplex. The natural multiplicative gradient flow on the simplex is the Shahshahani, or replicator, dynamics \cite{shahshahani1979},
\begin{equation}
\dot p_i
=
-p_i
\left(
\frac{\partial E}{\partial p_i}
-
\sum_jp_j\frac{\partial E}{\partial p_j}
\right).
\label{eq:replicator-gradient}
\end{equation}
Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:response}, this becomes
\begin{equation}
\dot p_i
=
p_i
(\mathbf a_i-\mathbf r)^{\mathsf T}
(\mathbf y-\mathbf r).
\label{eq:replicator}
\end{equation}
The overall adaptation rate has been absorbed into time. Equation~\eqref{eq:replicator} preserves Eq.~\eqref{eq:simplex} and leaves every boundary face invariant: a response with exactly zero weight is not recreated by redistribution alone.

\subsection{Variety as a response operator}

Define the weighted covariance of the response repertoire,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf C
=
\sum_i p_i
(\mathbf a_i-\mathbf r)
(\mathbf a_i-\mathbf r)^{\mathsf T}.
\label{eq:covariance}
\end{equation}
Differentiating Eq.~\eqref{eq:response} and using Eq.~\eqref{eq:replicator} gives the exact closed relation
\begin{equation}
\dot{\mathbf r}
=
\mathbf C(\mathbf y-\mathbf r).
\label{eq:response-dynamics}
\end{equation}
Thus the covariance of the available responses is also the linear operator that converts mismatch into motion of the realized response.

This gives the spectrum of $\mathbf C$ a direct dynamical meaning. If the system is initially matched, $\mathbf r_0=\mathbf y_0$, and the environment changes instantaneously by $\delta\mathbf y$, then
\begin{equation}
\dot{\mathbf r}(0^+)
=
\mathbf C_0\,\delta\mathbf y.
\label{eq:linear-response}
\end{equation}
The eigenvectors of $\mathbf C_0$ are therefore the principal directions in which the repertoire can initially respond, while the eigenvalues set the corresponding response rates. A direction in $\ker\mathbf C$ cannot be generated by an infinitesimal redistribution of the present repertoire.

For a fixed environment, Eq.~\eqref{eq:response-dynamics} also gives
\begin{equation}
\dot E
=
-(\mathbf r-\mathbf y)^{\mathsf T}
\mathbf C
(\mathbf r-\mathbf y)
\leq0.
\label{eq:error-decay}
\end{equation}
The same object therefore controls both the instantaneous capacity to respond to a change and the rate at which present mismatch can be removed.

\subsection{Reachable response geometry}

Because the weights remain on the simplex, every realized response lies in the convex hull
\begin{equation}
\mathcal R
=
\operatorname{conv}
\{\mathbf a_i:p_i(0)>0\}.
\label{eq:reachable-set}
\end{equation}
The set $\mathcal R$ is the global repertoire available to redistribution. Exact matching of a fixed target is possible only when $\mathbf y\in\mathcal R$; otherwise every allocation obeys
\begin{equation}
E
\geq
\frac12\operatorname{dist}^2(\mathbf y,\mathcal R).
\label{eq:distance-bound}
\end{equation}
This separates two notions that are often merged under the word variety. The convex set $\mathcal R$ determines which responses are representable at all, whereas $\mathbf C$ determines the local rate at which the current allocation can move through that set.

For the active repertoire, these two objects are linked exactly. Since $\mathbf C$ is positive semidefinite,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf v^{\mathsf T}\mathbf C\mathbf v
=
\sum_i p_i
\left[
\mathbf v^{\mathsf T}(\mathbf a_i-\mathbf r)
\right]^2.
\label{eq:covariance-quadratic}
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
\ker\mathbf C
=
\left[
\operatorname{span}
\{\mathbf a_i-\mathbf r:p_i>0\}
\right]^{\perp},
\label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
and therefore
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{rank}\mathbf C
=
\dim\operatorname{aff}
\{\mathbf a_i:p_i>0\}.
\label{eq:rank}
\end{equation}
The number of independent directions of immediate adaptation is exactly the affine dimension of the currently represented responses.

\subsection{Adaptation reshapes response variety}

The response operator is itself changed by adaptation. Introduce the third central moment
\begin{equation}
T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
=
\sum_i p_i
(a_{i\alpha}-r_\alpha)
(a_{i\beta}-r_\beta)
(a_{i\gamma}-r_\gamma).
\label{eq:third-moment}
\end{equation}
Differentiating Eq.~\eqref{eq:covariance}, the terms generated by the motion of the mean cancel, leaving
\begin{equation}
\dot C_{\alpha\beta}
=
\sum_{\gamma}
T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
(y_\gamma-r_\gamma).
\label{eq:covariance-dynamics}
\end{equation}
The current response is driven by the second moment of the repertoire, while the response operator is driven by its third moment. No monotonic law for total variety follows from the adaptation rule: depending on the shape of the repertoire relative to the environmental displacement, adaptation can increase some eigenvalues of $\mathbf C$ while decreasing others.

Equation~\eqref{eq:covariance-dynamics} also distinguishes representability from practical accessibility. For strictly positive initial weights, the deterministic dynamics does not remove a response in finite time, so the convex set $\mathcal R$ remains fixed. Nevertheless, weights can become arbitrarily small and the corresponding eigenvalues of $\mathbf C$ can collapse. The system can therefore retain a response globally while becoming increasingly slow at using it.

\subsection{Two-response limit}

The simplest case already shows this effect. Let the repertoire contain two responses $\mathbf a_1$ and $\mathbf a_2$, write
\begin{equation}
\mathbf d=\mathbf a_1-\mathbf a_2,
\qquad
p=p_1,
\end{equation}
and take a target on their affine line,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf y
=
\mathbf a_2+q\mathbf d.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\mathbf r
=
\mathbf a_2+p\mathbf d,
\qquad
\mathbf C
=
p(1-p)\mathbf d\mathbf d^{\mathsf T},
\label{eq:two-covariance}
\end{equation}
and Eq.~\eqref{eq:replicator} reduces to
\begin{equation}
\dot p
=
\|\mathbf d\|^2
p(1-p)(q-p).
\label{eq:two-response}
\end{equation}
If $0<q<1$, the target requires a mixture and $p\to q$, leaving a finite response variance $q(1-q)\mathbf d\mathbf d^{\mathsf T}$. If the target coincides with an extreme response, for example $q=1$, then $p\to1$ and
\begin{equation}
\mathbf C\to0.
\end{equation}
Adaptation to an extreme environment therefore produces specialization and simultaneously removes the local response rate to subsequent changes, even though the unused response has not been deleted from the model.

For $q=1$, let $\varepsilon=1-p$. Near specialization,
\begin{equation}
\dot\varepsilon
\simeq
-\|\mathbf d\|^2\varepsilon^2,
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon(t)
\sim
\frac{1}{\|\mathbf d\|^2t},
\qquad
\lambda_{\mathrm{resp}}(t)
=
p(1-p)\|\mathbf d\|^2
\sim
\frac{1}{t}.
\label{eq:specialization-rate}
\end{equation}
Here $\lambda_{\mathrm{resp}}$ is the single nonzero eigenvalue of $\mathbf C$. Prolonged adaptation to the same extreme response thus produces an algebraic loss of responsiveness to changes along the only available direction. This gives a minimal calculable tension between present adaptation and retained response capacity without introducing a separate diversity or resilience score.

The present model stops at redistribution within a fixed repertoire. It therefore addresses the retention and accessibility of existing responses, not the creation of new response vectors. Any extension toward knowledge creation must add a mechanism that changes the repertoire itself; Eqs.~\eqref{eq:response-dynamics}--\eqref{eq:covariance-dynamics} provide the baseline against which such a mechanism can be measured.
