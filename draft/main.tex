\title{Frequency-dependent fitness on genotype space}
\maketitle

A genotype fitness landscape assigns an intrinsic fitness $g_i$ to each genotype. This description is incomplete when the selective value of a genotype depends on the current population. We consider a minimal model in which mutation follows an explicit genotype graph while population composition changes relative fitness through a coupling matrix $A$. The aim is to determine when this feedback changes the mutational routes favored by selection.

\section{Model}

Consider binary genotypes $x_i \in \{0,1\}^L$, with frequencies $p_i \geq 0$ and $\sum_i p_i = 1$. Mutation connects genotypes at Hamming distance one. With mutation probability $\mu$, the mutation matrix is
\begin{equation}
Q_{ii} = 1 - \mu,
\qquad
Q_{ij} = \frac{\mu}{L}
\quad \text{if} \quad
d_H(x_i,x_j) = 1,
\end{equation}
and $Q_{ij} = 0$ otherwise.

The effective fitness of genotype $i$ is
\begin{equation}
f_i(p)
=
g_i
+
\beta \sum_j A_{ij} p_j.
\label{eq:fitness}
\end{equation}
Here $g_i$ is the intrinsic fitness and $A_{ij}$ measures how the frequency of genotype $j$ changes the fitness of genotype $i$. We therefore refer to $A$ as a fitness-coupling matrix.

Selection acts according to
\begin{equation}
\widetilde p_i
=
\frac{
p_i \exp[\eta f_i(p)]
}{
\sum_k p_k \exp[\eta f_k(p)]
},
\label{eq:selection}
\end{equation}
followed by mutation,
\begin{equation}
p_i(t+1)
=
\sum_j \widetilde p_j Q_{ji}.
\label{eq:mutation}
\end{equation}

The vector $g$, the matrix $A$, and the mutation operator $Q$ play distinct roles. The vector $g$ defines the intrinsic landscape, $Q$ defines which mutational moves are possible, and $A$ changes the relative selective value of those moves as the population changes.

\section{Relative fitness and local accessibility}

Only fitness differences enter the selection step. For two genotypes $i$ and $k$,
\begin{equation}
f_i(p) - f_k(p)
=
g_i - g_k
+
\beta \sum_j
(A_{ij} - A_{kj}) p_j.
\label{eq:relative-fitness}
\end{equation}
The first term is fixed by the intrinsic landscape, while the second depends on population composition.

Consider a population concentrated at genotype $i$ and a rare one-step mutant $j$. Before mutation mixing, the ratio $p_j/p_i$ is multiplied during selection by
\begin{equation}
\exp\left[
\eta
\left(
g_j - g_i
+
\beta (A_{ji} - A_{ii})
\right)
\right].
\end{equation}
The mutant therefore increases relative to the resident during the selection step when
\begin{equation}
g_j - g_i
+
\beta (A_{ji} - A_{ii})
>
0.
\label{eq:invasion}
\end{equation}

Equation~\eqref{eq:invasion} gives a local notion of accessibility in the rare-mutant limit. Mutation creates the mutant through $Q$, while the fitness coupling changes its selective effect after it appears. A step with $g_j < g_i$ can therefore become favorable in the population created by genotype $i$.

The matrix $A$ also contains a redundant component. Replacing
\begin{equation}
A
\longrightarrow
A + \mathbf{1} v^{\mathsf T}
\end{equation}
adds the same quantity $v^{\mathsf T}p$ to every genotype fitness and leaves Eq.~\eqref{eq:relative-fitness} unchanged. The dynamics therefore depends on differences between rows of $A$, not on all matrix entries independently.

\section{Minimal problem}

We first consider a rugged intrinsic landscape with a local peak and a higher peak elsewhere. Starting near the local peak, the baseline dynamics with $\beta = 0$ determines the mutational routes favored by the intrinsic landscape. We then introduce a low-dimensional family of fitness couplings and determine when Eq.~\eqref{eq:invasion} changes those routes.

A simple first family is
\begin{equation}
A_{ij}
=
a_{d_H(x_i,x_j)},
\label{eq:radial-coupling}
\end{equation}
where the coupling depends only on genetic distance. This keeps mutation and fitness coupling on the same genotype geometry and reduces the number of free coupling parameters from $O(4^L)$ to $O(L)$.

The first quantities to measure are the locally favorable mutational edges, escape from intrinsic local peaks, the resulting population trajectory, and their dependence on $\beta$, landscape ruggedness, and the coefficients $a_d$. Diversity is treated as an outcome of the dynamics rather than an optimization objective.

\section{Mathematical direction}

For the distance-dependent family in Eq.~\eqref{eq:radial-coupling}, both mutation and fitness coupling respect the symmetries of the hypercube and can be represented in the Walsh basis. This gives a complementary description in which mutation and frequency-dependent fitness act on the same genotype-space modes.

The local condition in Eq.~\eqref{eq:invasion} and the spectral representation address different scales of the same dynamics. The first describes how population feedback changes individual mutational steps near a resident state. The second can be used to determine how the same feedback changes relaxation or amplification of larger-scale variation across genotype space.
